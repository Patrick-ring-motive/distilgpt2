There are many **different properties of matter** [5]. Some materials are hard and brittle, while others are flexible [5]. Some materials are colourful, while others are transparent [5]. These kinds of features are called “properties” [5]. A cork floats on oil [5]. **Boiling point** is the hottest a liquid can get before becoming a gas [5]. **Freezing point** is the temperature at which a liquid becomes a solid [5]. **Brittleness**: Some materials, such as glass, are very brittle and will break when pushed out of shape [6]. Safety glass is designed to crack rather than break [6]. **Compressibility**: Gases can be squashed, or compressed, by squeezing more into the same space [6]. A bicycle pump pushes the particles closer together [6]. **Plasticity** is how well a solid can be reshaped [7]. **Conductivity** is how well a material lets electricity or heat travel through it [7]. **Malleability** is how well a solid can be shaped without breaking [7]. **Hardness**: A scientist called Friedrich Mohs created a scale of ten minerals to compare how hard they are [7]. Many materials are graded on this scale [7]. Diamond is the hardest mineral [7]. **Tensile strength** is how much a material can stretch without breaking [8]. **Flammability** is how easily and quickly a substance will catch fire [8]. **Density**: The amount of matter in a certain volume of an object is called its density [8]. Objects and liquids float on liquids of a higher density and sink through liquids of a lower density [8]. A plastic building brick sinks through oil but floats on water [8]. An onion sinks through oil and water, but floats on syrup [10]. Syrup sinks below water [10]. **Reflectivity** is how well a material reflects light [9]. Water reflects well [9]. **Transparency** is how well a material will let light pass through it [9]. **Flexibility** is how easily a material can be bent [9]. **Solubility** is how well a substance will dissolve, such as salt in water [10]. **A good insulator**: Heat cannot easily pass through some materials [10]. These are known as insulators [10]. For example, aerogel can completely block the heat of a flame [10]. **A smooth flow**: Some liquids flow more easily than others [11]. It depends on their “stickiness”, or viscosity [11]. Hot lava from a volcano flows slowly because it is sticky [11].

On 20 July 1969, **Neil Armstrong became the first person to walk on the surface of the moon** [11]. He was joined by Buzz Aldrin [11]. A third astronaut, Mike Collins, remained in orbit with the command and service modules [12]. Buzz Aldrin described the moon’s surface as like nothing on Earth [14]. He said it consisted of a fine, talcum-powder-like dust, strewn with pebbles and rocks [14]. The astronauts returned to Earth in the *Apollo 11* command module [14]. This fell through the atmosphere and landed in the Pacific Ocean [14]. A ringed float helped to keep it stable [14].
